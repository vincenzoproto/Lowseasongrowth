## Purpose

This note documents the source figures and calculations behind Low Season Growth Research Note 02 on international and domestic tourism demand growth in Europe during the first half of 2026.

It is intended to make the analysis transparent and reproducible. It is descriptive analysis, not a forecast or a benchmark for an individual hotel.

## Headline figures

First half of 2026:

- Total EU tourist-accommodation nights: **1.321 billion**
- Total year-on-year growth: **+1.7%**
- Foreign visitor share of nights: **48.9%**
- Foreign visitor night growth: **+2.5%**
- Domestic visitor night growth: **+0.9%**

Simple LSG comparison:

```
2.5 / 0.9 = 2.78
```

So international guest nights grew about **2.8 times faster** than domestic guest nights in percentage terms during the first half of 2026.

## Q1 contribution calculation

Eurostat reported:

- additional international nights: **11.4 million**
- additional domestic nights: **4.2 million**

LSG descriptive calculation:

```
11.4 / (11.4 + 4.2) = 0.7308
```

So approximately **73.1%** of the additional nights recorded in Q1 came from international visitors.

## Regional exposure to international demand — 2024 data

**Official evidence.** In 2024, foreign visitors generated more accommodation nights than domestic visitors in **65 of the 244 EU regions** covered by Eurostat, or **26.6%**. Foreign guests accounted for **94.4%** of nights in Kriti (Crete), **92.0%** in Jadranska Hrvatska and **90.2%** in Illes Balears. In contrast, **96.7%** of nights in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern came from domestic visitors.

**LSG interpretation.** National averages are a starting point, not a ready-made target-market strategy. For a weak period, compare the destination's demand mix with the guests your property actually attracts in those months before choosing an offer or channel.

**Apply it to your hotel.** Choose the months you want to strengthen. Review overnight stays by source market for the same months in previous years, then check current bookings and practical access to the destination. Treat any new target market as a hypothesis to test, not demand guaranteed by a regional share.

**Source and period.** Eurostat, *Regions in Europe — 2026 edition*, Tourism; published **23 September 2026**; reference year **2024**. The figures above, including 26.6%, are published Eurostat figures, not a new LSG indicator.

**Comparability limit.** Annual 2024 regional shares and H1/Q1 2026 growth indicators describe different periods and geographical levels; their comparison does not establish a time trend. The regional figures do not measure 2026 regional growth, booking windows, individual-property demand or demand for a particular low-season weekend. Practical recommendations are LSG interpretation, not findings measured by these statistics.

## Interpretation

The figures indicate that international demand contributed disproportionately to recent EU tourism growth, but exposure to foreign demand varies substantially by country.

This does **not** imply that every destination or hotel should target international guests. Property-level strategy should consider actual source markets, booking windows, access, channel mix, pricing, positioning and operational fit.

**Practical question:** Which source markets generated overnight stays at your property in the months you want to improve? Compare the same months in previous years, not only the annual average.
