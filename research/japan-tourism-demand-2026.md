## Purpose

This note documents a simple but commercially important pattern in Japan's 2026 tourism data: aggregate inbound demand can soften while selected source markets continue to set records.

The analysis uses official figures from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) and the Japan Tourism Agency.

It is descriptive analysis, not a forecast, destination ranking or benchmark for an individual hotel.

## Official source figures

### Inbound visitor arrivals

- July 2026 visitor arrivals: **3,442,100**
- July 2026 year-on-year growth: **+0.1%**
- August 2026 visitor arrivals: **3,098,900**
- August 2026 year-on-year growth: **-9.6%**
- **14 source markets** recorded their highest-ever August visitor arrivals in August 2026
- Italy and Spain recorded their **highest monthly visitor arrivals on record**

### Accommodation statistics

July 2026:
- Total guest nights: **54.67 million**
- Japanese guest nights: **41.10 million**
- Foreign guest nights: **13.58 million**
- Room occupancy rate: **60.8%**

June 2026:
- Total guest nights: **45.82 million**
- Japanese guest nights: **33.60 million**
- Foreign guest nights: **12.22 million**
- Room occupancy rate: **57.2%**

The Japan Tourism Agency notes that from January 2026 the Accommodation Travel Statistics Survey changed its stratification basis from number of employees to number of rooms, so year-on-year comparisons may include effects from that methodological change.

## LSG descriptive calculations

### July-to-August inbound change

```
3,098,900 - 3,442,100 = -343,200 visitors
```

```
(3,098,900 / 3,442,100 - 1) × 100 = -9.97%
```

So August visitor arrivals were about **10.0% lower than July** on a month-to-month basis.

### Foreign share of accommodation guest nights

July:

```
13.58 / 54.67 = 24.84%
```

June:

```
12.22 / 45.82 = 26.67%
```

### Occupancy movement from June to July

```
60.8% - 57.2% = +3.6 percentage points
```

These are Low Season Growth calculations from published JNTO / Japan Tourism Agency figures. They are not official indicators from those institutions.

## Interpretation

The August data shows why aggregate tourism growth should not be treated as a single demand signal.

Japan's total inbound arrivals fell year on year in August, yet 14 source markets still set August records. Italy and Spain reached all-time monthly highs.

For hotel commercial teams, the implication is straightforward: a weak or slowing total market does not mean every source market is weakening at the same time.

Source-market mix, flight capacity, holiday calendars, booking windows and traveller motivations can move differently.

This is especially relevant when planning shoulder periods or dates where domestic and regional demand may soften but selected long-haul markets remain strong.

## Commercial implications

1. **Do not use total arrivals as the only demand signal.** Source-market performance can diverge materially from the aggregate.
2. **Watch long-haul markets independently.** Italy and Spain setting all-time monthly records while total August arrivals declined illustrates why source-market segmentation matters.
3. **Separate accommodation occupancy from border arrivals.** Visitor arrivals and hotel guest nights measure different things and should not be treated as interchangeable.
4. **Use month-to-month movement carefully.** July-to-August changes can reflect seasonality, air capacity, weather and holiday timing.
5. **Respect methodology changes.** Japan's 2026 accommodation survey change means year-on-year accommodation comparisons require additional caution.
