## Purpose

This note documents selected seasonality and source-market figures from the **2026 Caribbean Travel Trends Report**, presented by Amadeus and the Caribbean Hotel and Tourism Association (CHTA).

The objective is to make the low-season implications easier to compare for hospitality professionals. This is descriptive analysis, not a forecast, destination ranking or benchmark for an individual hotel.

## Primary sources

- Amadeus Hospitality, *Caribbean Travel Trends 2026*  
  https://www.amadeus-hospitality.com/resources/caribbean-travel-trends-2026/

- Government of Antigua and Barbuda publication of the CHTA / Amadeus release, 13 May 2026  
  https://ab.gov.ag/detail_template.php?page=press_release/2026/may/2026_may_13-chta_and_amadeus_unveil_the_2026_caribbean_travel_trends_report

The CHTA / Amadeus release states that the report draws on Amadeus Travel Intelligence data covering **April 2025 through March 2026**.

## Official source figures

- Overseas demand to the Caribbean: **+1% year over year**
- Latin American source-market demand: **+24% year over year**
- Premium travel from South America: **+117% year over year**
- High-season hotel RevPAR: **US$183**
- High-season RevPAR growth: **+5.2% year over year**
- End-of-year holiday RevPAR: **US$283**
- Low-season hotel RevPAR: **US$125**
- Brazil low-season arrivals: **+60%**
- Colombia low-season arrivals: **+26%**
- CARIFESTA XV 2025 arrivals in Barbados: **+23%**
- Intra-Caribbean share during the festival period: **23.3%**, up **3.3 percentage points**

## LSG descriptive calculations

### High-season vs low-season RevPAR gap

```
183 - 125 = US$58
```

Low-season RevPAR was about:

```
58 / 183 = 31.7%
```

below the reported high-season RevPAR level.

Equivalently, high-season RevPAR was about **46.4% higher** than the low-season level.

### Holiday vs low-season RevPAR gap

```
283 - 125 = US$158
```

Low-season RevPAR was about:

```
158 / 283 = 55.8%
```

below the reported end-of-year holiday RevPAR level.

These percentages are Low Season Growth calculations from the published CHTA / Amadeus figures. They are not official Amadeus or CHTA indicators.

## Interpretation

The data illustrates two separate but related patterns.

First, hotel revenue performance remains materially weaker in the reported low-season period than during high season and year-end holidays.

Second, source-market diversification is already contributing to off-peak demand. Brazil and Colombia recorded strong low-season arrival growth, while Latin American demand overall grew substantially faster than total overseas demand to the Caribbean.

This does not prove that every Caribbean hotel should target the same markets. Property-level strategy should still consider air access, destination positioning, booking windows, rates, product fit, channel mix and actual source-market demand.

## Limitations

- The figures are regional and do not represent every island, destination or property equally.
- RevPAR values reported for broad periods do not reveal the distribution across individual hotels.
- Source-market growth rates do not measure profitability or booking conversion.
- The report period spans April 2025 through March 2026; figures should not be interpreted as a full calendar-year 2026 forecast.
- The LSG gap percentages are simple descriptive calculations and do not establish causality.
